Answers to the Guideline 2.1 information request.

1. SCREEN RECORDING: Attached. Recorded on an iPhone 15 Pro, iOS 26.6. Begins with launching the app and shows: live PCC trolley positions on the G Line, route directions from a Metro and a Regional Rail station, turning on Trolley Alerts (including the iOS notification permission prompt, the only permission the app requests), saving a stop alert, analytics, the share sheet, the About page, adding and tapping the home screen widget, and offline mode in Airplane Mode. No accounts, login, registration, in-app purchases, subscriptions, or user-generated content.

2. DEVICES TESTED: iPhone 15 Pro, iOS 26.6 (physical, TestFlight and Xcode). iPhone 17 Pro and 17 Pro Max Simulators, iOS 26.5, Xcode 26.6. Minimum iOS 17.0. iPhone only.

3. WHAT IT DOES: Philly Trolleys shows where SEPTA's vintage 1940s PCC trolley cars are running on the G Line (Girard Avenue) in Philadelphia right now. The PCC cars share the route with buses and do not run every day, so riders cannot tell from SEPTA's own tools whether a trolley or a bus is coming. The app tracks the trolleys specifically: which cars are out and where each is heading, directions to the line from any SEPTA Metro or Regional Rail station with live times, optional notifications when trolleys start running or near a saved stop, a home and lock screen widget with the count of cars out, analytics (typical hours, busiest days, car roster, service history), and offline mode showing the last data seen. Audience: Philadelphia transit riders, tourists, rail enthusiasts. Rated 4+. One-time paid app; no ads, accounts, tracking, or in-app purchases.

4. SETUP: No login, credentials, or sample files. Open the app; live positions load within seconds. PCC cars usually run about 6 AM to 8 PM Eastern; outside those hours the home screen says no PCC cars are out and lists the buses covering the route. Route directions, alerts, widget, analytics, and offline mode work at any hour. Alerts: scroll to Trolley Alerts, turn on the switch, allow notifications, pick first car of the day or every car, optionally save a stop, direction, and distance. Widget: long press the home screen, plus button, search "Philly Trolleys", add small or medium. It refreshes about every 10 minutes, when the app opens, and when the Updated line on the widget is tapped. Offline: with no connection the app shows the last positions and analytics it saw, labeled with their time. Share, About, Privacy, and Support are in the footer.

5. EXTERNAL SERVICES: SEPTA public transit data APIs (www3.septa.org/api: TransitView, NextToArrive, Arrivals, Stops, BusSchedules) and SEPTA GTFS schedule data. Netlify hosts the app's web content and the serverless functions that fetch and cache SEPTA data, compute analytics, and send notifications, plus the optional support form. Supabase (hosted Postgres) stores the trolley sighting log behind the analytics and, only after a user turns on alerts, the device push token and saved stop alerts. Apple Push Notification service, called directly from our server (no third-party push provider). Capacitor (Ionic) is the native shell. No authentication service, payment processor, advertising or analytics SDK, or AI service.

6. REGIONS: No differences. Offered in the United States only, content specific to Philadelphia, functions the same everywhere.

7. REGULATED INDUSTRY OR PROTECTED MATERIAL: Not a regulated industry. Vehicle positions and schedules come from SEPTA's public, documented developer data feeds. The app is independent, is not affiliated with SEPTA, and says so in the app, the store description, and the website. SEPTA's name appears only descriptively; the app name, icon, and artwork are original.
